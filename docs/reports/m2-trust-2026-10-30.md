# M2 trust report: the reference core (2026-10-30)

**Verdict: the core is trustworthy enough to continue to M3.** It matched every original-derived
record we could replay, and survived 9.9 million ticks of random input after one fix. This
does not prove parity: no original runtime was executed, and large areas have no independent
record to compare against.

Binary pin for all original-side reads: SHA-256 `27e61475…a6a07f02`, re-verified on this date.

## 1. Comparison with the original's recorded behaviour

The bundle's author ran the pinned `DaybreakDX.exe` handlers under Unicorn with controlled input
and kept the results. `sim_oracle_test` replays the same conditions through our compiled bodies.

| Record | Result | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Admission at melee rows 300-302 | **1,600/1,600**, including 334/334 state requests | 46 combos, sampled DAT contexts, 5 input patterns |
| Admission at shot rows 400-412 | **14,400/14,400**, including 1,007/1,007 state requests | 46 combos, 800 contexts, 18 input patterns |
| State-400 body, normal | **17,028/17,028** | 43 combos, 6 forced sections, frames 0-65 |
| State-400 body, special | **17,028/17,028** | Same, with the special mode active |

The body rows compare speed, offset, movement, gravity, scratch fields, the frame clock and the
ordered event list (models, effects, voices, attack counts, shots with their angles).

**Sensitivity.** Replaying admission on the wrong weapon gives 1,584/1,600, and a wrong RNG value
in the body replay gives 15,756/17,028, so both comparisons can fail. The admission control is
weak: most sampled rows do not depend on the weapon.

**Excluded from the body comparison, with the reason:**

- `fire_world` x and z: zero in the record because the emulated cosine table was zeroed
  (`roster_ground_shot400_world.hpp` documents this).
- `fire_attachment`'s last argument where it holds the emulated actor's address.
- Motion and scratch on the 119 rows per trace that transition, compared only up to the
  transition, because the record stubs those services and the body does not.

**Independence limit.** The generated bodies come from the same binary, so this shows that
generation and our build reproduce those handlers under those inputs. It does not test the
surrounding integration, which is hand-written by the author.

## 2. Random input

`scripts/sim_trust.py` ran `simfuzz` on all 46 combos: 30 runs of up to 7,200 ticks each, about
9.9M ticks, with two fuzzed players, two CPUs and varied rosters and stages. Same arguments give
the same result.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Process crashes | 0 |
| Exceptions | 10 on the first run, **0 after the fix below** |
| Non-finite position or heading, out of world | 0 |
| HP outside its range, gauge outside 0..1000 | 0 |
| Stuck or frozen actor | 42 reports across two rows, all on CPU slots (below) |

**Defect found and fixed.** Matches containing Ohishi+2 threw "Legacy angle outside defined
conversion range". The disassembly at `0x4dbed5` on the pinned binary shows the original itself
builds a pitch near 2^32 there: it adds a negative field to `rand()%30` and loads the sum with
`FILD QWORD` and a zeroed high word. The original then converts through `_ftol2`
(`FUN_0066b668`), which keeps the low 32 bits, so nothing fails. The guard was the error, not the
value. The fix is the first entry in `src/sim/core/PATCHES.md`, with two regressions that failed
first. It is evidence from code, not from observing the original run.

**CPU holding a shot, open.** A CPU can sit in row 450 (or once, 350) with its clock frozen for
over 30 seconds. All 42 reports are on CPU slots, no human was affected, and it ends. The recovered
CPU (`keiichi_cpu.hpp:89-91`) only releases a held shot under distance and line-of-sight conditions,
which fits a CPU waiting for a clear shot, but I did not prove that is the cause or compare it with
the original. Worst case it is a passive-CPU annoyance to check in the playtest.

Row 124's speed of 10 per tick was **not** confirmed cleanly: steps ranged from 0 to 25.7, since

103/203 and 133 unreachable for Rena+0 from its shipped data, but in the core row 121 follows hits on
a lying victim, 103/203 follow airborne cases, and 133 was reached. The port's scan only looked at
records naming those rows; the core enters them through other paths.

## 4. Coverage

**55.0% of declared rows reached** (3,217 of 5,847 combo-row pairs), from 46% (Akane+2) to 63%
(Rika+2). Only slot 0's rows count.

| Family | Reached / declared |
|---|---|
| 0-99, movement | 591 / 723 |
| 100-199, front reactions | 1,112 / 2,172 |
| 200-299, back reactions | 476 / 770 |
| 300-399, melee and specials | 647 / 1,117 |
| 400-499, shots | 352 / 881 |
| 500+, activations | 39 / 184 |

56 rows were never reached on any combo: 11, 12, 28, 109-112, 117, 122, 123, 125-127, 129, 132, 135,
186, 188, 232, 233, 303, 304, 321, 335, 337, 338, 342-344, 349, 352, 359, 362, 371, 393, 398, 399,
404, 405, 407, 414-417, 421-423, 454, 455, 459, 464, 465, 468, 499, 502, 503. A declared row is not
proof of a reachable row, and the fuzzer does not build gauges on purpose or use the hyper chord
deliberately, so this is a floor. Treat unreached rows as untested, not as broken.

## 5. What this does not establish

- **No natural-match comparison.** Every original record has forced or synthetic conditions.
- **No independent record** for dash, jump, melee or full-charge bodies, or for hit, damage,
  projectile and CPU integration, beyond the sample in §3.
- **Rena's shots are not compared.** All three Rena combos use hand-written shot code rather than
  generated bodies, so the state-400 traces do not cover them. Her admission rows are covered.
- **Match endings are thin.** Only 8 of 1,380 runs finished within 7,200 ticks, so result, victory
  and award paths ran little.
- **One machine and architecture.** Determinism was checked with g++ and clang on x86-64.
